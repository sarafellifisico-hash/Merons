The Compalpha and its Rotating Interaction

Within TUIDG, Alpha Components—or Compalphas (Cα)—are not material objects. The framework begins from the ontological premise that matter and energy are not substances essentially distinct from nothingness, understood here as absolutely empty space. A Compalpha is therefore described as a purely geometric structure with a toroidal form.

Its visual structure is expressed by an infinite family of circular lines called Interaction Circles (CI). These circles project from a common Alpha Point (αP) through infinitely many spatial directions, forming the continuous geometry represented in this simulation.

Rotating Interaction (RI) is the geometric rule relating these circles through apparent rotation. Their relative rotational agreement determines the resulting interaction:

Concordant rotationGeometric attraction
Discordant rotationGeometric repulsion

No separate material substance is exchanged between the Compalphas; in the model, attraction and repulsion arise from the relative geometric configuration of their Interaction Circles.

According to their range and relative interaction intensity, the Interaction Circles are classified as follows:

Meronic and Intrameronic Circles · CM / CiM Within TUIDG, CM are associated with Meronic formation and CiM with Meronic stabilization. Together they are proposed as the Meronic-scale counterpart of the strong interaction described by conventional particle physics.
Molecular and Intramolecular Circles · CMo / CiMo CMo are associated with molecular formation and CiMo with molecular stabilization. Together they are proposed as the TUIDG counterpart of the quantum-electromagnetic effects that conventional physics uses to describe chemical bonding and intermolecular stability.
Exomolecular and Intraexomolecular Circles · CEx / CiEx CEx are associated with the formation of macroscopic bodies and CiEx with their stabilization. Together they are proposed as the TUIDG counterpart of gravitational interaction at macroscopic and astronomical scales.

These designations identify different scales of one and the same geometric interaction—RI—rather than distinct mechanisms or different kinds of material entity.

Didactic-scale note. The separations used in this presentation—Med = 1 Md, Mod = 24 Md and Exd = 96 Md—and their displayed relative force magnitudes are normalized visualization parameters. They are not experimentally calibrated lengths or measured force strengths. In conventional physics, the strong, quantum-electromagnetic and gravitational regimes have different ranges, coupling behavior and mathematical descriptions; molecular binding is not classified as a separate fundamental force, and gravity is not included in the Standard Model. The animation therefore communicates TUIDG’s proposed geometric hierarchy qualitatively. Its real distance ratios and interaction intensities may differ and require quantitative definition and experimental comparison.
