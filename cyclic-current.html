Electricity Generation and the Mechanism of Cyclic Interaction

Explanatory text for the interactive presentation

How can a rotating magnet generate electricity in a copper loop?

In conventional physics, which recognizes the existence of particles, this phenomenon is described by electromagnetic induction: a change in the magnetic flux through the loop produces a voltage that drives electrons through a closed circuit, establishing a current of these particles. The mechanical energy used to turn the rotor is thus converted into electrical energy.

TUIDG proposes a geometric interpretation of this process. In this description, conductors and magnets are not made up of particles such as protons and electrons, but of toroidal fields called Compalphas, interconnected through their Interaction Circles. Here, magnetism corresponds to the collective orientation of the Alpha Axes of these Compalphas, and electric current corresponds to the transmission of the oscillations of these Alpha Axes through this geometric network.

Thus, when the magnets pass close to the loop, their interaction causes the Alpha Axes of the Compalphas represented in the copper to twist. As the magnets move away, these axes return to their initial orientations. The repetition of this movement represents the excitation of Cyclic Interaction, whose transmission through the circuit is interpreted, in TUIDG, as Cyclic Current.

In this simulation, the induction window makes visible the angular interval during which the Compalphas twist and return. The LED flashes and the voltmeter pulses follow this movement. The window is a demonstration parameter, and the voltage values are illustrative.

This presentation makes it possible to visualize the mechanism proposed by TUIDG and compare it with the conventional description of electromagnetic induction. The animation expresses this geometric proposal, whose physical validation still requires quantitative relationships and comparison with experiments.
